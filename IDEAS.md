# Early ideas

> Brainstorm from the start of the project. **What we built:** a 3D version of the Constellation (2) with Ripples of influence (4), where time is depth. See the README.


The "map" is a map of **relationships and meaning**, not of time or place. Dates stay on each story, but they never organize the view.

## 1. Theme lines (transit-style map)
- Each theme is a colored line: *Healing Atlanta*, *Building Black Business*, *Faith & Community*, *Justice & Memory*, *The Founders*.
- Each person is a station.
- People who belong to two themes are **interchange stations**. Ride any line, change at any interchange.
- **Why it works:** everyone already knows how to read a transit map. It shows that one life spans several stories.
- **Example:** Grace Towns Hamilton is an interchange of *Healing Atlanta* (Hughes Spalding Hospital) and *Civic Leadership* (Urban League, first Black woman in the Georgia legislature).

## 2. Constellation
- People are stars, sized by how much of their story is documented. Lines are real, sourced ties: family, church, school, co-founders, colleagues.
- Clusters form naturally (founders, medicine, faith).
- Click a star and its connections light up.
- **Why it works:** it shows South-View as a community, not a list of graves. People are remembered as stars.
- **Risk:** gets crowded past ~60 people, so it needs a theme filter.

## 3. Memory quilt
- Each story is a patch, colored by theme.
- Clicking a patch opens it, and the patches it connects to light up.
- New stories add new patches, so the quilt grows.
- **Why it works:** quilting is a deep African American tradition of keeping memory, and the quilt grows like the cemetery does.
- **Must ask the partner first:** using a cultural form carries meaning. South-View should approve the metaphor.

## 4. Ripples of influence
- One person sits at the center. Rings show reach: family, then church and neighborhood, then Atlanta institutions, then the nation.
- Click anyone on a ring to re-center the map on them.
- **Why it works:** it answers "why does this person matter?" visually.
- **Risk:** it can turn into ranking people by "impact," which conflicts with dignity for everyday lives.

## Recommendation
**Theme lines (1)** as the main view:
- Clearest in a 5-minute pitch, and the easiest to usability-test ("find Dr. Yancey, then switch to another line").
- Non-linear by design. It uses the partner-named figures directly and grows by adding stations and lines.

**Constellation (2)** as the "see everything" view later. It uses the same people and the same connections.
