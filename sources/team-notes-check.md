# Checking the team notes

The places where [team-notes.md](team-notes.md) disagreed with South-View's documents, checked against an outside source on 2026-10-20 (#1–3) and 2026-11-01 (#4–5).

| # | Question | Team notes | South-View document | Outside source says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | David T. Howard's birth year | 1849, Knoxville, Georgia | Brochure: "(1819-1935)". Script: 1849, Crawford County | Born 1849 in Crawford County, Georgia, citing his 1935 *Atlanta Daily World* obituary ([National Center for Civil and Human Rights](https://www.civilandhumanrights.org/?p=138529)). Knoxville is the Crawford County seat, so the two places agree. | **Notes are right.** The brochure's 1819 is a typo. No change. |
| 2 | R. C. Robson's gift to Howard | $200 | Script: $150 | Nothing found online. It would take the 1882 will or a newspaper notice of it. | **Unresolved.** Leave the amount off the map. |
| 3 | Lewis Watts | Albert's **son** Lewis Gould Watts, secretary until 1936 | Dinah Watts Pace script: "our brother Lewis was the first secretary" | Albert's son Lewis "became secretary of the organization after his death and filled this role until his own death in 1936" ([Royster & Mehta, *Building Memories*, Georgia Tech](https://leading-edge.iac.gatech.edu/building-memories/southview-cemetery)). Separately, Dinah had a brother Lewis Watts, a Pullman porter; her parents were Emily and Sterling Watts, the same parents the notes give Albert ([Wikipedia, citing her 1933 death certificate and *The Covington News*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinah_Watts_Pace)). | **Notes are right.** There were two men named Lewis Watts. The script merges them by calling the brother "the first secretary". No change to the notes. |
| 4 | Irene Dobbs Jackson's library card year | 1961 | — | Henderson, *South-View*, p. 216: the Atlanta Public Library board agreed to desegregate after a federal suit filed in March 1959, and she then received her card. The Atlanta 100 and the African American Registry also give 1959 (May 1959). The card's own source, *American Libraries* "By the Numbers: Atlanta" (2017), says 1959. | **Notes are wrong.** Card, Dates and Year on map changed to 1959 on 2026-11-01. Dates set to 1908–1999 (Henderson p. 216 gives d. 1999; [Amistad Research Center](https://amistad-finding-aids.tulane.edu/agents/people/268) gives 1908–1999). |
| 5 | Where Irene Dobbs Jackson rests | Not stated (assumed South-View under ADR 0004) | Women of the Movement flyer includes her | Henderson lists her in the biographical dictionary of South-View residents (p. 216) and quotes her epitaph among South-View's literary epitaphs (p. 129). | **Confirmed.** Moved from "assumed" to "confirmed by a source" in ADR 0004. |

## Caveats

- **#1:** The NCCHR page wouldn't open when I tried to read it. The 1849 / Crawford County / *Atlanta Daily World* details come from search results that quote it.
- **#3:** *Building Memories* may share its research with the book behind the team notes, so it's an independent check, but not a strong one.
- **#3:** The Wikipedia article on Dinah Watts Pace is a secondary source.
