## Dr. King's Birthday Becomes a National Holiday (1983)

Sources:
1. "What Goes into Making a Federal Holiday: Ronald Reagan and Martin Luther King, Jr. Day." Ronald Reagan Presidential Library, National Archives, 16 Jan. 2017, https://reagan.blogs.archives.gov/2017/01/16/what-goes-into-making-a-federal-holiday-ronald-reagan-and-martin-luther-king-jr-day.
2. "Reagan Signs King Holiday." United Press International, 2 Nov. 1983, https://www.upi.com/Archives/1983/11/02/Reagan-signs-King-holiday/1735436597200/.
3. "How the Martin Luther King Jr. Birthday Became a Holiday." *Constitution Daily*, National Constitution Center, 20 Jan. 2025, https://constitutioncenter.org/amp/blog/how-martin-luther-king-jr-s-birthday-became-a-holiday-3.
4. Henderson, D. L. *South-View: An African American City of the Dead*. Carrelspin Press, 2018, pp. 114–15, 220.

South-View Residents Involved
* Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. – First laid to rest at South-View, 1968–1970 (pg. 114–15).
* Rev. Martin Luther King Sr. and Alberta Williams King – Dr. King's parents, who rest at South-View in the crypt where their son first rested (pg. 115, 220).

Historical Notes
* Four days after Dr. King was assassinated in 1968, Representative John Conyers of Michigan introduced the first bill to make King's birthday a federal holiday. (Source 3)
* The bill that finally passed, H.R. 3706, was introduced by Representative Katie Hall of Indiana. (Source 1)
* President Ronald Reagan signed it on November 2, 1983, in the White House Rose Garden, with Coretta Scott King and about two hundred civil rights leaders present. It set the holiday on the third Monday in January. (Sources 1, 2)
* The first federal King holiday was observed in 1986. By 2000, all fifty states observed it as a state holiday. (Source 3)

Narrative for website

Four days after Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. was assassinated in 1968, Michigan congressman John Conyers introduced the first bill to make King's birthday a national holiday ("How the Martin Luther King Jr. Birthday Became a Holiday"). It took fifteen years. On November 2, 1983, President Ronald Reagan signed the bill in the White House Rose Garden, with Coretta Scott King and about two hundred civil rights leaders looking on, and set the holiday on the third Monday in January ("Reagan Signs King Holiday"; "What Goes into Making a Federal Holiday"). The nation first observed it in 1986, but some states held out, and not until 2000 did every state observe it ("How the Martin Luther King Jr. Birthday Became a Holiday"). Dr. King was first laid to rest at South-View in 1968, and his parents, Rev. Martin Luther King Sr. and Alberta Williams King, rest there today (pg. 114–15).

## Forsyth County Brotherhood Marches (1987)

Sources:
1. "Brotherhood March." *New Georgia Encyclopedia*, https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/brotherhood-march/.
2. Dodd, Sophia. "Remembering the Brotherhood March: 35 Years Later." Atlanta History Center, https://www.atlantahistorycenter.com/exhibitions/remembering-the-brotherhood-march-35-years-later/.
3. Little, Becky. "In 1912, This Georgia County Drove Out Every Black Resident." History.com, A&E Television Networks, 23 May 2018, https://www.history.com/articles/georgia-racial-expulsion-stacey-abrams.
4. "Focus on Issues: They Came to March for Brotherhood." Jewish Telegraphic Agency, 30 Jan. 1987, https://www.jta.org/archive/focus-on-issues-they-came-to-march-for-brotherhood.

South-View Residents Involved
* John Lewis – Marched in the second Brotherhood March as a U.S. congressman (Source 1).

Historical Notes
* In 1912, white residents of Forsyth County drove out all 1,098 of the county's Black residents. (Source 3; Source 2 says "more than 1,000")
* No Black people lived in the county in 1980. (Source 2)
* Two local residents and Atlanta civil rights leader Hosea Williams organized the march, which was planned around the new Martin Luther King Jr. holiday. (Sources 1, 2)
* On January 17, 1987, about 75 marchers, Black and white, set out for the courthouse in Cumming. Counter-protesters, some in Ku Klux Klan robes, threw rocks, bottles and bricks, and the organizers called off the march at the sheriff's request. (Sources 1, 2)
* On January 24, 1987, more than 15,000 people marched. Speakers included Hosea Williams and Coretta Scott King. Marchers included Congressman John Lewis. (Source 1)
* Nearly half of the January 24 marchers were white. (Source 4)
* Williams led a third march in 1988 with 161 people. (Source 2)

Narrative for website

In 1912, white residents of Forsyth County, north of Atlanta, drove out all 1,098 of the county's Black residents, and for decades afterward the county kept Black people out ("In 1912, This Georgia County Drove Out Every Black Resident"; "Remembering the Brotherhood March"). In January 1987, as the nation marked its second King holiday, local residents and Atlanta civil rights leader Hosea Williams organized a Brotherhood March there ("Brotherhood March"). On January 17, about seventy-five marchers, Black and white, set out for the courthouse in Cumming; counter-protesters, some in Klan robes, threw rocks, bottles and bricks until the march was called off ("Brotherhood March"; "Remembering the Brotherhood March"). A week later, on January 24, more than fifteen thousand people returned, among them Coretta Scott King and Congressman John Lewis, who now rests at South-View ("Brotherhood March"). Williams led a third march in 1988 ("Remembering the Brotherhood March").

## 1906 Massacre Centennial Remembrance (2006)

Sources:
1. Henderson, D. L. *South-View: An African American City of the Dead*. Carrelspin Press, 2018, pp. 119–20.
2. "Reasons to Celebrate Black History Month: The 1906 Atlanta Riot." *The Atlanta Journal-Constitution*, https://ajc.com/lifestyles/reasons-celebrate-black-history-month-the-1906-atlanta-riot/kbBasrl5ynpbBjsrNwPixM/.
3. Auchmutey, Jim. "Atlanta 1906 Race Riot Remembered: City's Bloody Stain Seen with New Eyes." *The Atlanta Journal-Constitution*, 14 Jan. 2006, https://www.ajc.com/news/atlanta-1906-race-riot-remembered-citys-bloody-stain-seen-with-new-eyes/PWIJXP7XT5FRBJ5EAJ7K6MLSXI/.
4. "Atlanta Marks Centennial of Deadly Race Riot." Voice of America, 21 Sept. 2006, https://www.voanews.com/a/a-13-2006-09-21-voa46/399420.html.
5. Justice, Elaine. "Faculty Instrumental in Atlanta Race Riot Centennial Remembrance." *Emory Report*, vol. 59, no. 4, 18 Sept. 2006, https://news.emory.edu/emory-report-archive/erarchive/2006/September/September%2018/RaceRiot.htm.
6. Coalition to Remember the 1906 Atlanta Race Riot. "Centennial Remembrance Weekend." Program, 21–24 Sept. 2006, eGrove, University of Mississippi, https://egrove.olemiss.edu/atrr_docs/6.
7. "Atlanta Race Massacre Remembrance." *Rough Draft Atlanta*, 20 Sept. 2024, https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2024/09/20/atlanta-race-massacre-remembrance/.

South-View Residents Involved
* George Union Wilder and Frank Smith – Victims of the massacre whose graves at South-View have been found (pg. 119).
* The other Black victims buried at South-View – Burial records show ten in all (pg. 119).

Historical Notes
* For most of the century the massacre went unmarked. Historian Clarissa Myrick-Harris told the AJC that the Black community also wanted to put it behind them and move forward. (Source 3)
* For the hundredth anniversary, civic leaders, historians, genealogists and educators formed the Coalition to Remember the 1906 Atlanta Race Riot. (Sources 4, 5)
* The coalition's Centennial Remembrance Weekend ran September 21–24, 2006, at sites around Atlanta. (Sources 5, 6)
* On Thursday, September 21, 2006, a memorial service was held at South-View for the African American victims. (pg. 120)
* The AJC records a memorial service at the old Ebenezer Baptist Church, followed by a funeral procession to South-View. (Source 2)
* According to burial records, ten African American victims were buried at South-View; only the graves of George Wilder and Frank Smith have been located. (pg. 119)
* Remembrance events at South-View are now part of the annual commemoration, including a theatrical tour and the Praise House Project in 2024. (Source 7)

Narrative for website

For most of the twentieth century, Atlanta said little about the 1906 massacre, and many Black families also chose to put it behind them ("Atlanta 1906 Race Riot Remembered"). As the hundredth anniversary approached, civic leaders, historians, genealogists and educators formed the Coalition to Remember the 1906 Atlanta Race Riot and planned a Centennial Remembrance Weekend for September 21–24, 2006 ("Atlanta Marks Centennial"; "Centennial Remembrance Weekend"). On Thursday, September 21, a memorial service was held at South-View for the Black victims of the massacre (pg. 120). Mourners also gathered at the old Ebenezer Baptist Church and followed a funeral procession to the cemetery ("Reasons to Celebrate Black History Month"). Burial records show that ten Black victims were laid to rest at South-View in 1906, though only the graves of George Wilder and Frank Smith have been found (pg. 119). Remembrance at South-View has continued since; in 2024 the annual commemoration included a theatrical tour of the cemetery and the Praise House Project ("Atlanta Race Massacre Remembrance").

## John Lewis Laid to Rest at South-View (2020)

Sources:
1. "America Paid Homage to Civil Rights Icon John Lewis This Week in Multi-City Homegoing Worthy of a Head of State." *Culture Type*, 31 July 2020, https://www.culturetype.com/2020/07/31/america-paid-homage-to-civil-rights-icon-john-lewis-this-week-in-multi-city-homegoing-worthy-of-a-head-of-state/.

3. "John Lewis: A Founding Father of a Better America." *The Atlanta Journal-Constitution*, 30 July 2020, https://www.ajc.com/john-lewis/john-lewis-a-founding-father-of-a-better-america/2PYQEPHYIRH7NE4R6B5SS2JPZU/.
4. Moye, J. T. "John Lewis (1940–2020)." *New Georgia Encyclopedia*, 20 July 2020, https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/government-politics/john-lewis-1940-2020/.
5. Henderson, D. L. *South-View: An African American City of the Dead*. Carrelspin Press, 2018, p. 223.

South-View Residents Involved
* John Lewis – Laid to rest at South-View on July 30, 2020 (Sources 1, 3). His own card tells his life in full.
* Lillian Miles Lewis – His wife, beside whom he rests (Source 3; pg. 223).

Historical Notes
* Lewis chaired SNCC, was beaten by Alabama state troopers on the Edmund Pettus Bridge in 1965, and represented Georgia's Fifth District in Congress for more than thirty years. (Source 4)
* He died on July 17, 2020. His "Celebration of Life" ran six days, July 25–30, in Alabama, Washington and Georgia. (Source 1)
* On July 26, his casket crossed the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma on a horse-drawn caisson. (Source 1)
* He was the first Black lawmaker to lie in state in the Rotunda of the U.S. Capitol. (Sources 1, 2)
* On July 30, three former presidents honored him at his funeral at Ebenezer Baptist Church. (Sources 2, 3)
* After the funeral he was buried at South-View next to his wife, Lillian, who died in 2012. (Sources 1, 3)

Narrative for website

John Lewis (1940–2020) chaired the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee, was beaten by Alabama state troopers as he led marchers across the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma in 1965, and later represented Atlanta in Congress for more than thirty years (Moye). After he died on July 17, 2020, his casket crossed that same bridge on a horse-drawn caisson, and he became the first Black lawmaker to lie in state in the Rotunda of the U.S. Capitol ("America Paid Homage"; "Former Presidents Pay Tribute"). On July 30, after a funeral at Ebenezer Baptist Church where three former presidents honored him, he was laid to rest at South-View beside his wife, Lillian Miles Lewis ("John Lewis: A Founding Father"; pg. 223). His own card tells the full story of his life in the movement.

## Hank Aaron Laid to Rest at South-View (2021)

Sources:

South-View Residents Involved
* Henry "Hank" Aaron – Laid to rest at South-View in 2021; moved to his permanent tomb in 2022 (Sources 2, 3). His own card tells his life in full.
* Rev. Samuel W. Williams – Former pastor of Friendship Baptist Church, where Aaron's funeral was held (Source 1; pg. 254).
* Rev. Martin Luther King Sr. and Alberta Williams King – Their crypt is near Aaron's tomb (Source 4).

Historical Notes
* On April 8, 1974, in Atlanta, Aaron hit his 715th home run and broke Babe Ruth's record, after a winter of racist hate mail and threats. (Source 5)
* He died in January 2021, at 86. (Source 3)
* His funeral was held on January 27, 2021, at Friendship Baptist Church. (Source 1)
* On the way to South-View, his procession turned onto Hank Aaron Drive and stopped at the marker for his 715th home run at the site of the old Atlanta–Fulton County Stadium. (Source 2)
* He was placed in a temporary crypt at South-View until his permanent resting place was finished. (Sources 2, 3)
* On January 5, 2022, he was moved to his permanent tomb, near the crypt of Dr. King's parents. (Sources 3, 4)

Narrative for website

Henry "Hank" Aaron (1934–2021) broke Babe Ruth's home run record in Atlanta on April 8, 1974, after a winter of racist hate mail and threats ("Watch: Hank Aaron"). He died in January 2021, at eighty-six ("Hank Aaron Laid to Rest at Permanent Gravesite"). On January 27 his funeral was held at Friendship Baptist Church, once led by Rev. Samuel W. Williams, who also rests at South-View ("Hank Aaron's Funeral"; pg. 254). On the way to the cemetery, his funeral procession stopped at the marker for his 715th home run, at the site of the old Atlanta–Fulton County Stadium ("Hank Aaron Hearse Makes Stop"). He rested in a temporary crypt at South-View while his tomb was finished, and on January 5, 2022, he was laid in his final resting place near the crypt of Rev. Martin Luther King Sr. and Alberta Williams King ("Braves Legend Hank Aaron Moved"). His own card and the 715th Home Run card tell the rest of his story.

## 1906 Massacre Victims Identified (2023)

Sources:
1. Palmore, Kaci. "Unearthing the Past: Identifying Victims of the 1906 Atlanta Race Massacre." National Center for Civil and Human Rights, 14 Sept. 2023, https://www.civilandhumanrights.org/unearthing-the-past-identifying-victims-of-the-1906-atlanta-race-massacre/.
2. "Two Victims in 1906 Atlanta Race Massacre Identified, Nearly 120 Years Later." WSB-TV, 18 Sept. 2023, https://www.wsbtv.com/news/local/atlanta/two-victims-1906-atlanta-race-massacre-identified-nearly-120-years-later/5LVMJY55DFBPZP76CKLWA57T5Y/.
3. "New Victims Identified in 1906 Atlanta Race Massacre." Axios Atlanta, 19 Sept. 2023, https://www.axios.com/local/atlanta/2023/09/19/atlanta-race-massacre-victims.
4. "Klibanoff Helps Identify Two Victims of 1906 Atlanta Race Massacre." Emory History News, Emory University, 30 Oct. 2023, https://scholarblogs.emory.edu/emoryhistorynews/2023/10/30/klibanoff-helps-identify-two-victims-of-1906-atlanta-race-massacre/.

South-View Residents Involved
* Marshall Carter – A 13-year-old schoolboy; identified in 2023 (Source 1).
* Stinson Ferguson – A 25-year-old resident of Thurmond Street; identified in 2023 (Source 1).

Historical Notes
* Researchers with the National Center for Civil and Human Rights' Truth and Transformation Initiative, the Georgia Civil Rights Cold Cases Project at Emory University, and the Afro-American Historical and Genealogical Society found original Fulton County Board of Health death certificates for the two victims. (Sources 1, 2, 4)
* The certificates use the word "riot," the period's term for the massacre. (Source 1)
* Marshall Carter, 13, a schoolboy, lived with his family on Love Street near Summerhill. Stinson Ferguson, 25, lived on Thurmond Street in northwest Atlanta. Both died on the first night of the massacre, September 22, 1906, and both were buried at South-View. (Source 1)
* The names were made public in September 2023, the week of the 117th anniversary. (Sources 2, 3)
* WSB-TV reported them as two of at least nine unknown victims. (Source 2)
* The researchers sent the names to the Equal Justice Initiative, so they can replace "unknown" entries on its list of racial terror lynchings in Fulton County. (Source 1)

Narrative for website

For more than a century, some of the Black Atlantans killed in the 1906 massacre were remembered only as "unknown" ("Two Victims in 1906 Atlanta Race Massacre Identified"). In September 2023, researchers from the National Center for Civil and Human Rights, Emory University's Georgia Civil Rights Cold Cases Project, and the Afro-American Historical and Genealogical Society announced two more names, found in Fulton County death certificates ("Unearthing the Past"; "Klibanoff Helps Identify Two Victims"). Marshall Carter was a thirteen-year-old schoolboy who lived with his family on Love Street near Summerhill, and Stinson Ferguson, twenty-five, lived on Thurmond Street ("Unearthing the Past"). The white mob killed both on the first night of the massacre, September 22, 1906, and both were laid to rest at South-View ("Unearthing the Past"; "New Victims Identified"). The researchers sent their names to the Equal Justice Initiative so that they can replace "unknown" on its record of racial terror lynchings in Fulton County ("Unearthing the Past").

## South-View Listed on the National Register (2026)

Sources:
1. "Weekly List 2026 07 10." National Register of Historic Places, National Park Service, 10 July 2026, https://home.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/weekly-list-2026-07-10.htm.
2. "National Register of Historic Places Adds 4 Georgia Landmarks." FOX 5 Atlanta, 1 Oct. 2026, https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/national-register-historic-places-adds-4-georgia-landmarks.
3. "Advocates Begin Process to Include South View Cemetery on Historic Places List." Axios Atlanta, 19 July 2024, https://www.axios.com/local/atlanta/2024/07/19/advocates-begin-process-to-include-south-view-cemetery-on-historic-places-list.
4. "Historic South-View Cemetery in Atlanta Seeks National Recognition." *Rough Draft Atlanta*, 5 May 2026, https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2026/05/05/south-view-cemetery-historic-atlanta/.
5. "Cemetery Established by Formerly Enslaved Black Atlantans Applying for Historic Status." *The Atlanta Journal-Constitution*, June 2024, https://www.ajc.com/news/south-view-cemetery-applying-for-historic-recognition/PGJTI6ZETNGEVCNDON6OUDGZAY/.
6. Henderson, D. L. *South-View: An African American City of the Dead*. Carrelspin Press, 2018, pp. 1, 11, 13.

South-View Residents Involved
* Albert Watts and the other five founders – Founded South-View in 1886 (pg. 13).
* Everyone who rests at South-View.

Historical Notes
* South-View's founders created the cemetery after Black families were mistreated at Oakland and Westview cemeteries. (pg. 1, 11, 13)
* In 2024, city leaders, the Atlanta Preservation Center and the South-View Cemetery Association began the nomination, aiming for a listing by the cemetery's 140th anniversary in 2026. (Sources 3, 5)
* By May 2026, South-View was listed on the Georgia Register of Historic Places, and National Register staff were preparing the final nomination package for Washington. (Source 4)
* The National Park Service listed South-View on the National Register on July 6, 2026 (reference number SG100013193). (Source 1)
* The Atlanta Preservation Center sponsored the nomination. South-View spans 101 acres and holds about 80,000 graves. It was the first privately owned Black cemetery corporation in post–Civil War Georgia. (Source 2)
* With the listing, South-View joins Oakland and Westview on the National Register. (Source 3)

Narrative for website

In 1886, Albert Watts and five other Black Atlantans founded South-View because the city's cemeteries, including Oakland and Westview, segregated and neglected Black burials (pg. 1, 11, 13). In 2024, the Atlanta Preservation Center and the South-View Cemetery Association began working to place the cemetery on the National Register of Historic Places in time for its 140th anniversary ("Advocates Begin Process"; "Cemetery Established by Formerly Enslaved Black Atlantans"). By May 2026 South-View was on the Georgia Register of Historic Places, and on July 6, 2026, the National Park Service added it to the National Register, where it now stands beside Oakland and Westview ("Historic South-View Cemetery in Atlanta Seeks National Recognition"; "Weekly List 2026 07 10"; "Advocates Begin Process"). Today it covers 101 acres and holds about 80,000 graves, and it was the first privately owned Black cemetery corporation in Georgia after the Civil War ("National Register of Historic Places Adds 4 Georgia Landmarks").

## Discrepancies (not for the cards)

1. **King holiday signing date.** The Reagan Library, its photo records and UPI give November 2, 1983. A King Day timeline gives November 3, and the text of a 2012 White House archive post says November 20 (its own photo caption says November 2). The card uses November 2.
2. **Forsyth march sizes.** The first march: about 75 (Atlanta History Center) or about 90 (JTA). The second: "over 15,000" (*New Georgia Encyclopedia*, Atlanta History Center) or about 20,000 (Wikipedia). The card says "about seventy-five" and "more than fifteen thousand."
3. **Forsyth 1912 number.** 1,098 (History.com, from the 1910 census), "more than 1,000" (Atlanta History Center), about 1,100 (NPR). These agree; the card uses 1,098.
4. **The 2006 service.** Henderson (pg. 120) places a memorial service at South-View on Thursday, September 21, 2006. The AJC describes a service at the old Ebenezer Baptist Church followed by a funeral procession to South-View, with no date. The card gives both without saying they were the same day.
5. **Victims buried at South-View.** Henderson says burial records show ten (pg. 119); the AJC says "at least eight." The card uses Henderson.
6. **Society name.** The NCCHR article says "Afro-American Genealogical Historical Society (AAGHS)"; WSB-TV says "Afro-American Historical and Genealogical Society of Metro Atlanta." The national group is the Afro-American Historical and Genealogical Society, which the card uses.
7. **National Register date.** The NPS weekly list says South-View was listed on July 6, 2026; FOX 5 says July 8. The card uses the NPS date.
8. **Size of South-View.** FOX 5 (2026): 101 acres, about 80,000 graves. Axios (2024): over 100 acres, more than 90,000 people. The card uses FOX 5, which matches CBS News and WSB-TV (80,000).
9. **Founders.** Axios (2024) says "nine Black businessmen." Henderson and South-View's own brochure say six (see ledger flag 3). The card says six.
10. **Lewis lying in state.** VOA and Culture Type: the first Black lawmaker to lie in state in the U.S. Capitol Rotunda. An AJC piece calls him only the fourth Black person to lie in state there. Both can be true; the card says "first Black lawmaker."
