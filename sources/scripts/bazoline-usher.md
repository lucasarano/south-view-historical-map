**<u>Bazoline Usher</u>**

*Have you learned something new today? After hearing all these wonderful stories from my fellow residents, what have you learned? Every day offers a new opportunity to expand your mind, pick up a new skill, or gain knowledge from different perspectives. The pursuit of knowledge is a lifelong journey and I should know…. I lived to be 106! *

*You got a minute? (wait for crowd response) Oh great! Let me tell you a little about my life…*

*I was born December 26, 1885, in Walnut Grove, GA as Basil Estelle Usher. My mother loved cooking with basil and named me after the herb. As a precocious teenager, I decided that Basil didn’t suit me – too masculine – so I changed my name to Bazoline.*

*My parents valued education so much that they moved our family to Covington, GA to attend better schools. In Covington, Mrs. Dinah Watts Pace was one of my teachers. She told my parents that I was an adept learner and recommended I matriculate to Atlanta University’s laboratory high school. At the age of* 14, I moved to Atlanta to attend Lab High and continued to the University. AU had an outstanding teaching faculty. Dr. WEB DuBois was one of the most gifted thinkers that I had ever met. Did you know that Dr. DuBois was left-handed? …just like me. When I saw what a brilliant scholar Dr. DuBois was, I thought maybe left-handedness was alright for me too. I graduated from AU in 1906,,,,,,the only woman in a class of 9 students.

*After graduation I accepted a teaching position in Virginia but returned to Atlanta after ten years. I taught in several Atlanta Public Schools and later became principal of David T. Howard Junior High. *

*In 1944, I was appointed director and supervisor of black schools in Atlanta - the highest position an African-American had ever attained in the Atlanta School system and I was the first black to have an office at Atlanta City Hall. I consulted teachers and educators at Atlanta University, NCA&T, and Fort Valley State College. I consider training teachers to be one of my greatest contributions. ”You have to know how children learn in order to teach. If you know how people learn, then you can make progress.” *

*I was an active member of Friendship Baptist church and taught Sunday School to many of the youth-- including future Mayor, Maynard Jackson. I was also a member of the Kappa Omega chapter of Alpha Kappa Alpha sorority. *

*Dedicated to my career, I never married or had children of my own. After my sister Inez passed, I adopted my niece, Lavada when she was 11. She became a kindergarten teacher herself. *

*In 1943, Grace Towns Hamilton and I started the first black Girl Scout Troop in Atlanta at Friendship Baptist and recruited 30 black teachers to be troop leaders across the city. I absolutely loved the Girl Scouts.* Girl Scouts broke ethnic boundaries! It’s commitment to instilling in girls’ courage, confidence, and character makes our world a better place.  I continued to pay my Girl Scout dues until my death and I received their highest honor, the “Thank You” pin. In 1989, the Atlanta Girl Scout Council honored me for a century of service to others. Watching the girls thrive and grow to become contributing citizens of the world was one of my greatest joys. 

*I retired from the Atlanta school system in 1954. The Harwell Road Elementary School in Collier Heights was renamed the Bazoline Usher Middle School in my honor in 1988. I died February 8, 1992, at the age of 106. Posthumously, I was awarded the Georgia Woman of Achievement. *

*So there you have it…lifelong learning is my secret to a fulfilling life. Learning enriches not just your life, but the world around you. Now go along now…learn everything that you can!*
