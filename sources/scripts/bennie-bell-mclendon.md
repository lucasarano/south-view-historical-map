*Scene – Standing in the front room of her house dressed for the Opera, Mrs. McLendon is waiting for her ride to the Fox Theatre in 1962.*

Hello everyone! *(Looking a little confused)* Thank you for stopping by. I’m on my way out, so I just have a minute to speak with you. I’m headed to the annual performance of the NY Metropolitan Opera at the Fox Theatre.

I am Mrs. Bennie Bell McLendon, married to Dr. Frederick Earl McLendon and secretary of the McLendon Medical Clinic. My husband and I started a 40 bed private hospital in 1946 located in the Hunter Hills neighborhood of Atlanta, you know over near Mosley Park. When we opened our hospital there were over 100,000 Negros living in Atlanta, but only 44 hospital beds to serve them! Grady would only see charity cases. If you were Negro and got sick in Atlanta - you had to leave the state or die!

The McLendon Medical Clinic was outfitted with the finest surgical apparatus available. It had physio-therapy, obstetrics and X-Ray departments and was the model for other black hospitals around the country. We attracted Negro doctors to Atlanta and provided a hospital for them to train and work. We delivered more babies than we can count! Improving health care for our community was our passion. The clinic’s letterhead read, “Good Health is an Envied and Priceless Possession”.

Dr. McLendon called me the “brain power” behind the hospital. My title was Secretary, but I handle all the financial records, the patient records, the appointment records and the day-to-day activities. I did just about everything other than direct patient care. We had a booming and growing facility!

My husband is a graduate of Morehouse College and of Meharry Medical School. He completed his residency at Homer G. Phillips Hospital in St. Louis and set up practice in Athens before moving to Atlanta. Ebony Magazine featured us an article about the clinic! They called Dr. McLendon a “Militant Medic” because of his involvement in the Civil Rights Movement. Earl offered free medical care to the protestors injured at bloody Sunday – the protest in Selma, Alabama. After a meeting of the Atlanta Medical Association, of which he was president, he and several other doctors staged a sit in at the Biltmore Hotel’s segregated café. They were all arrested, but we **must** stand up for what we believe in or there won’t **be** any progress!

Last year, 1961, my good friend, Dorothy Alexander and I bought tickets to the Metropolitan Opera’s annual performance at the Fox Theatre. Dorothy is a fashion designer, and we put on our finest gowns and took a car to the Fox …only to get turned away! They said our tickets were for seats in the White Section of the theatre! Can you believe that? We were incensed! We protested to get the seating desegregated. The Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee and the Southern Christian Leadership Conference sent telegrams on our behalf to the director of the New York Metropolitan Opera. One was co-signed by Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. himself. Our efforts seemed to work!

This year, the seating is officially desegregated! I missed the performance last year, so I can’t wait to see it tonight! The singing, the sets, the costumes, the ambiance…. such an enchanting experience! Maybe more of **us** will attend the opera without Jim Crow seating interfering! It’s the small indignities that cut so deep. We have our own schools and churches, communities, and social clubs, but when we interact with the White community, we are treated like …. well… like we are less than human. Why should it matter what race sits beside another at the opera?

For there to be equality between the races**, everyone** has a role to play. **You** have a role to play too -even today! Oh ---There is my ride! (runs out beyond crowd then turns and addresses them again ) Remember my friends - Everyone deserves to be treated with dignity - every day! I’m counting on you to do your part!
