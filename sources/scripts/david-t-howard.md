“For as much as it has pleased almighty God to take out of this world this soul, we therefore commit this body to the ground, earth to earth, ashes to ashes, dust to dust looking for that blessed hope, and the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the Archangel and with the trumpet of God, and the dead in Christ shall rise first.”

Good Afternoon! I am David Tobias Howard, proprietor of D.T. Howard & Company – one could argue, the most successful undertaking establishment in the city. Successful, in part, because of my keen business acumen and commitment to honest and fair practices. “My three rules of business are: first be square to all, taking advantage of none, second, always keep a promise: and third, never tell a lie or make a business bluff!”

I was born a slave in 1849 in Crawford County, Georgia, and was trained as a young boy to be a body servant for Col. T.C. Howard. In 1865, I came to Atlanta and worked as a waiter for a couple years at the Exchange Hotel on the corner of Alabama and Pryor streets before becoming a Pullman Porter. Then, I was a personal assistant to railroad boss, Mr. R.C. Robson until his death in 1882.

Because of my loyalty and devotion, Mr. Robson gifted me \$150 in his Will and I did what any enterprising young gentleman would do…I launched my entrepreneurial career. Both Col. Howard and Mr. Robson told me “There is nothing like a man being his own boss…”. “I have never attended a day of school or had any education, but I’ve always paid attention to older people…always attended to business!”

I opened my first location on Walton and Broad streets near where the Citizens and Southern Bank building was. I bet you can’t guess how much rent I paid for this prime location back then…\*waits for a couple of responses before continuing\* Oh, you’re never gonna guess…\$6 a month! \*winks\* Didn’t I tell you I was a shrewd businessman! I also won a city contract for burying negro paupers right here at South-View by submitting a bid of \$1.24 per body and underbidding two other undertakers, one whose bid was double my cost. See my motto was to “Treat Everyone Right” and this included rich and poor folks.

But y’all are here because you wanna know about the Race Massacre, is that right? Well, let me tell you I’ve seen a lot of death in my profession but not as much as I witnessed during those awful few days in September 1906. \*pause for few seconds to reflect\* Bodies were scattered through the downtown streets from the terror of those white men. We collected bodies all night along Decatur Street and cut some down from lampposts. The horror was indescribable – bodies that were bludgeon and shot and stabbed. I believe the official death count was 25 blacks and 2 whites. It is rumored that I buried a lot of white victims that weren’t counted... some right here at South-View… but that’s all I’m gonna say about that.

In 1923, I donated 7.5 acres for an all-black elementary school before the Atlanta Public School System took it over and named it David T. Howard High in 1948. I’m sure you may have heard of some of the school’s illustrious alumni like Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr., NBA player Walt Fraiser, Olympic gold medalist Mildred Singleton, Mayor Maynard Jackson, and builder Herman Russell. I’m proud to say that the school is now a middle school dedicated to nurturing young business leaders for the future.

I married my dear wife, Ella Branner in 1870 and together we had five children who all helped in the family business. I served as president and chairman of the Southview Board of Directors and buried some of the cemetery’s first residents. I became a resident here myself in April 1935 and received many tributes in the city for not only being a business leader but a respected leader in the community.

“Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air, and we shall we ever be with the Lord, wherefore comfort ye one another with these words.”
